origin

I spent three years at Bechtel, working up from Junior Software Engineer to Software Engineer — shipping production systems at enterprise scale, fixing what broke, building what didn't exist yet. It was steady work, and it was going somewhere. But the parts of engineering that actually pulled at me — AI, machine learning, the systems underneath the systems — weren't the parts my day job asked for. So I left, and started again at TU Dresden. Not because anything failed. Because zero felt like the right place to rebuild from.

roots

India's ancient history keeps surprising me the more I dig into it — Takshashila was teaching students around 600 BCE, roughly seventeen centuries before Oxford existed, and Nalanda ran as a full residential university for nearly 750 years, drawing scholars from as far as China, Korea, and Tibet. I think the same instinct that pulls me toward old systems — wanting to know how something worked, what made it endure, why it eventually didn't — is the same one that makes debugging code nobody's touched in years genuinely interesting to me. The Ramayana and Mahabharata pull at me for a similar reason: some of the oldest continuously retold stories in human history, still being rewritten into something new every generation. Living in Germany has only sharpened that curiosity outward — Dresden has its own centuries buried under it too, and I'm slowly learning to look for them.
